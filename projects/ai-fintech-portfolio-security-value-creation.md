## Evidence-grounded premise

NIST CSF 2.0 gives organizations a governance-centered taxonomy for cybersecurity outcomes. NIST's GenAI Profile identifies risks unique to or amplified by generative AI. These sources support portfolio value creation that treats security and AI governance as product, distribution, resilience, and valuation drivers rather than compliance costs alone.

## Case approach

- Baseline each company against governance, identity, data, model, software-supply-chain, detection, response, and recovery outcomes.
- Prioritize control gaps by customer harm, revenue dependency, regulatory exposure, and enterprise-sales friction.
- Tie remediation to product roadmaps, customer evidence, and accountable executives.
- Track improvement through repeatable profiles and independent evidence rather than self-reported maturity labels.

## Evidence-based success measures

Use critical-gap closure, incident containment, recovery testing, enterprise diligence findings, AI evaluation coverage, and revenue blockers removed. These are proposed measures, not historical results.
