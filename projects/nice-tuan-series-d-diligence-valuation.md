# Nice Tuan: Reconstructing an Investment Decision After the Market Turned

I led a post-investment review of Nice Tuan for an investment audience. I wanted to understand how a grocery service could look attractive to mass-market shoppers and investors while its local operations remained fragile. The people in the decision system were customers, community sellers, suppliers, delivery operators, company leaders, regulators, and investors.

This was a 2023 diligence exercise completed during my Rakuten experience, not participation in Nice Tuan's financing or management. My deliverable was a forensic investment case: rebuild what an investor could reasonably have known, identify the assumptions that should have controlled valuation, and turn the failure into a better monitoring design.

## The public record I would have put in front of the committee

The company, also known as Shi Hui Tuan, used a WeChat mini-program to aggregate neighborhood grocery orders. That model could lower purchase and last-mile costs, but it also placed demand generation, local fulfillment, supplier settlement, and price subsidy inside the same cash cycle.

The chronology showed why funding scale could not stand in for operating proof:

- **2020:** Nice Tuan raised almost $450 million across four rounds, according to AgFunder's contemporaneous financing record.
- **March 2021:** it raised another $750 million in a round co-led by Alibaba and DST Global, bringing reported funding since inception above $1.2 billion.
- **May 2021:** China's State Administration for Market Regulation found below-cost selling and misleading price comparisons, imposed a RMB 1.5 million fine, and ordered a three-day suspension in Jiangsu. The notice included specific products sold below recorded purchase cost.
- **August 2021:** TechNode reported city closures, layoffs, unavailable ordering in Shanghai, and a retrenchment toward selected provinces after the company described some regions as heavily loss-making.
- **March–April 2022:** multiple reports said nationwide operations had stopped and the company was settling supplier and employee obligations. One contemporaneous report also cited a reporter who found limited residual activity, so I treat “complete shutdown” as reported rather than independently verified fact.

That sequence invalidated a simple “large market plus strong sponsors equals durable winner” thesis. It did not, by itself, reveal the private unit economics. My review therefore separated public facts from the model inputs an investor would still have needed.

## I changed the unit of diligence from company growth to a mature city cohort

Gross merchandise value could rise when the company entered more cities or increased subsidy. Neither proved that a local market would produce cash. I rebuilt the thesis around a city after its launch incentives began to normalize:

`mature-city contribution = customer gross profit − consumer subsidy − community-seller incentive − picking and delivery cost − spoilage and refunds − local overhead`

I then tested six links in the chain:

1. **Demand without subsidy:** order frequency, basket size, and retained households after promotions fell.
2. **Density economics:** whether delivery and picking cost declined as a route matured, rather than merely as volume rose.
3. **Supplier cash cycle:** payment terms, inventory loss, and cash required before a new city reached steady contribution.
4. **Community-seller durability:** retention and productivity of the local people who acquired customers and handled pickup.
5. **Competitive response:** the price and service response available to larger platforms with deeper balance sheets and existing traffic.
6. **Regulatory permission:** whether the pricing needed to win a market remained lawful and repeatable.

The official enforcement notice made the sixth question measurable. When products are sold below documented purchase cost and the remedy includes suspension, pricing conduct is not a footnote to valuation; it is evidence that the acquisition engine may be unusable.

## The valuation bridge I would have required

I separated observable facts, management-provided operating data, and investor assumptions. IFRS 13 was useful as a discipline—not because this review produced an accounting fair value, but because it requires market-participant assumptions, makes unobservable inputs visible, and treats uncertainty as part of the measurement.

The base case used only mature-city cohorts that demonstrated positive contribution after normalized subsidy. The downside case increased customer attrition, fulfillment cost, supplier cash need, and regulatory constraint together. Expansion received no terminal value credit until a new city repeated the mature-cohort pattern.

Capital would have been released in tranches against five gates:

| Gate | Evidence before more capital | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Retention after subsidy normalization | Orders disappear when discounts stop |
| City economics | Positive cohort contribution including full fulfillment cost | GMV grows while cash contribution worsens |
| Cash | Supplier payments and working capital remain within plan | Stretching payables finances expansion |
| Conduct | Pricing and promotion pass local regulatory review | Growth depends on below-cost or misleading offers |
| Replication | New city approaches the mature-city curve | Each launch needs more time or subsidy than the last |

## What the review changed

The output was not a claim that I predicted Nice Tuan's collapse. It was a reusable diligence standard for subsidy-led marketplaces. I would discount reported scale until retention, contribution, working capital, and regulatory permission improved in the same cohort. I would also begin post-close monitoring with the same variables used to justify entry, so a deteriorating thesis could not hide behind a new growth metric.

The central investment lesson is precise: capital can accelerate a working local engine, but it can also hide an engine that has never worked without capital. The distinction belongs in the underwriting model before it appears in a shutdown report.
