# Nice Tuan: reconstructing an investment decision after the market turned

This was a 2023 forensic diligence exercise during my Rakuten experience—not participation in Nice Tuan’s financing or management.

My mandate was to reconstruct the decision without hindsight theater: separate what an investor could have known at entry from what collapse later revealed, identify which assumptions should have controlled valuation, and decide whether another dollar could rationally follow the original round.

## What the committee would have known publicly

Nice Tuan / Shi Hui Tuan aggregated neighborhood grocery orders through a WeChat mini-program. The model promised lower procurement and last-mile costs but joined customer subsidy, community-seller incentives, supplier settlement, and local fulfillment inside one fragile cash cycle.

The chronology challenged the headline-growth thesis:

- **2020:** almost $450 million across four rounds, per contemporaneous AgFunder reporting.
- **March 2021:** another $750 million, co-led by Alibaba and DST Global; reported funding since inception above $1.2 billion.
- **May 2021:** China’s market regulator found below-cost selling and misleading price comparisons, imposed a RMB 1.5 million fine, and ordered a three-day Jiangsu suspension.
- **August 2021:** city closures, layoffs, unavailable Shanghai ordering, and retrenchment toward selected provinces were reported.
- **March–April 2022:** reports said nationwide operations had stopped while supplier/employee obligations were settled, though one contemporaneous report found limited residual activity.

Large rounds and strong sponsors were observable. Durable private unit economics were not.

## I changed the unit of underwriting

Company GMV could rise through new cities or deeper subsidy. Neither proved a local market created cash.

The diligence unit became a mature city after launch incentives normalized:

**customer gross profit − consumer subsidy − seller incentive − picking/delivery − spoilage/refunds − local overhead = mature-city contribution**

Six questions controlled value:

1. Do households retain frequency and basket after discounts fall?
2. Does route density lower picking/delivery cost in mature cohorts?
3. Can supplier terms and working capital support replication?
4. Do community sellers retain and remain productive?
5. Can the city withstand response from better-capitalized platforms?
6. Is the acquisition/pricing engine legally repeatable?

The regulator’s below-cost finding made the sixth question inseparable from valuation. If the conduct required to acquire a city is prohibited, growth spend is not a scalable asset.

## The valuation bridge

I separated three evidence classes:

- observable public fact;
- management-provided operating data;
- investor assumption.

[IFRS 13](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-13-fair-value-measurement/) supplied useful discipline around market-participant assumptions, input visibility, risk, and sensitivity; this review was not an accounting fair-value opinion.

The base case valued only mature cities with positive contribution after normalized subsidy. The downside moved attrition, fulfillment cost, supplier cash need, and regulatory restriction together. Expansion received no terminal-value credit until new cities repeated the mature curve.

## Five capital-release gates

| Gate | Evidence required | Stop |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Retention after subsidy normalization | Orders disappear with discounts |
| City economics | Positive full-cost contribution | GMV rises while cash contribution worsens |
| Cash | Supplier settlement and working capital inside plan | Stretching payables funds growth |
| Conduct | Local approval of pricing/promotion | Below-cost or misleading offers remain essential |
| Replication | New city converges toward mature-city curve | Each launch consumes more time or subsidy |

This changed both entry and monitoring. The same variables justifying valuation would govern follow-on capital, so a deteriorating thesis could not hide behind a newly selected growth metric.

## Investment conclusion

The output was not a claim that I predicted collapse. It was a decision standard for subsidy-led marketplaces:

- discount scale until normalized retention and full-cost contribution coexist;
- model working-capital and supplier stress as part of growth;
- treat conduct risk as a constraint on the acquisition engine;
- give replication value only after cohorts converge;
- stage capital against falsifiable gates.

The central lesson: capital can accelerate a working local engine or conceal one that never worked without capital. The underwriting model must distinguish those states before the shutdown report does.
